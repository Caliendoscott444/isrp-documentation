# Punishment Guide

This guide provides a consistent framework for reviewing community rule violations and recording moderation decisions.

!!! warning "Management approval required"
    This is an initial ISRP draft. The original Jackson County punishment guide was not available to inspect. The exact offense list, penalty ladder, strike thresholds, appeal deadlines, and staff authorization levels must be confirmed by ISRP management before enforcement.

## 1. Core principles

All moderation should be:

- **Consistent:** Similar situations should receive comparable consideration.
- **Proportionate:** The response should reflect the seriousness and impact of the conduct.
- **Evidence-based:** Staff should review available context before deciding.
- **Documented:** Formal actions should be recorded accurately.
- **Impartial:** Personal disagreements must not determine an outcome.

## 2. Review process

1. Identify the reported conduct and the rule that may apply.
2. Review the available messages, clips, screenshots, witness statements, or other relevant context.
3. Give the member an opportunity to provide relevant context when appropriate.
4. Consider severity, repetition, impact, prior documented actions, and any mitigating circumstances.
5. Select an action permitted by the current ISRP policy and the staff member's authorization.
6. Explain the decision and record the action in the approved staff log.
7. Refer uncertain, disputed, or higher-level matters to management.

## 3. Possible moderation responses

The following are response categories, not a finalized ISRP penalty ladder:

| Response | General use |
|---|---|
| Verbal reminder | A low-level concern that can reasonably be corrected through guidance |
| Warning | A documented notice that a rule was violated |
| Temporary restriction | A time-limited restriction where permitted by ISRP policy |
| Removal from a session | Used when continued participation is disrupting an activity |
| Suspension | A more serious or repeated matter reviewed under management policy |
| Ban or removal | Reserved for conduct meeting the approved threshold and authorization rules |

Management must define exact durations, thresholds, and which staff ranks may issue each action.

## 4. Factors to consider

Staff should consider the full context, including:

- The rule involved and how clearly it applies.
- Whether the conduct was intentional or repeated.
- The effect on other members or the session.
- Relevant prior, documented moderation history.
- Whether the member acknowledged the issue or attempted to correct it.
- Whether the available evidence is reliable and complete.

Prior actions should not be assumed; staff should verify the record.

## 5. Documentation requirements

A formal record should include, where applicable:

- Member's Discord username and user ID.
- Staff member responsible for the action.
- Date and time.
- Rule or policy involved.
- Summary of the incident.
- Evidence links or attachments.
- Action taken and its duration, if applicable.
- Reviewing manager, if required.
- Appeal or review outcome.

Records must be factual, professional, and limited to information relevant to the case. Do not publish private case information in public channels.

## 6. Staff authorization and escalation

Staff may only issue actions they are authorized to issue under the current ISRP chain of command and moderation permissions.

Escalate a case when:

- The appropriate action is unclear.
- The staff member is personally involved.
- The case concerns another staff member.
- The proposed action exceeds the staff member's authority.
- Evidence is disputed or the case may require management review.

## 7. Appeals and reviews

Members should use the official ISRP appeal process. An appeal should identify the action being challenged and explain why a review is requested.

The reviewing staff member should, where practical, be different from the person who made the original decision. Reviewers should consider the original evidence, the member's explanation, and whether policy was applied correctly.

An appeal may be upheld, modified, or overturned under the final ISRP appeal policy. Submitting an appeal does not guarantee that an action will be changed.

## 8. Case integrity

- Do not fabricate, alter, or selectively misrepresent evidence.
- Do not retaliate against someone for submitting a good-faith report or appeal.
- Do not discuss confidential cases outside authorized channels.
- Correct inaccurate records through the approved management process.
